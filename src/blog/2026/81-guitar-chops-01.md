A couple of months ago, I got lost in Starfield. I didn't do much else, and I hardly played my guitar. It's only been a kind of "secondary hobby" since about 2015, so that's hardly surprising, and the amount of time spent playing has been fluctuating a lot that whole time.

Around that time, a dear old friend (and former band mate) approached me with a suggestion: let's each pick a riff/phrase/part of a song in turns, work on that for a week or two, then send a video recording to each other. A simple way to get out of one's comfort zone, explore something one would otherwise not have looked into, and maybe learn something new - I loved that idea!

We're still at it, my motivation to play has significantly increased, and so has my actual time spent playing.

What we did so far:

- The "Are You Gonna Go My Way" riff (Lenny Kravitz)
- The intro riff of "Crazy Train" (Ozzy Osbourne)
- The iconic "Voodoo Child" riff (Jimi Hendrix)
- The first 12 bars of "Crossroads" (Cream)

Increased interest in playing lead to [a new guitar](/blog/a-new-guitar/), that I upgraded with a Pre-Wired ST Pickguard Tex-Mex and a set of Gotoh S11 saddles last week (picture: [Strat after upgrade](/img/blog/guitars/strat2.jpg)).

Installing the pickguard was fairly easy, but the first test gave me half a heart attack - hardly any output at all. I immediately checked the output jack, all good, then wiggled the pickguard out from under the strings, checked everything again, but didn't notice anything wrong. Plugged the guitar into the amp once again, 1 of 3 pickups with normal output levels. Disassembled again, and finally realized that something must be touching ground somewhere. After a bit of measuring and comparison with the old pickguard, I came to the conclusion that the bottom of the 5 way switch must be touching the body cavity. And that cavity is painted with conductive paint. So, I glued a piece of duct tape under the 5 way switch, tested again, and it all was good - I really didn't need that hour of disappointment and panic, but I'm happy that everything worked out in the end.

Other than that, I'm catching up on all the music theory that I skipped a long time ago. Most importantly, getting out of the "box shape tunnel vision" and into intervallic functions, exploring modes, (properly) learning triads, and working on melodies/harmonies/phrasing for lead guitar playing. All of that is _much_ easier with a looper - I'm still glad I got myself one last year, and I now believe it's an essential pedal for any guitarist.
