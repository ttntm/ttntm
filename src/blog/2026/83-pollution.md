Woke up. Went to gym. Lifted weights.

Came back home. Showered. Had coffee.

Turned on computer. Replied to some work stuff. Scrolled through some feeds.

Highlights include "No way to find what I'm looking for, $service full of gen AI to prompt new slop, despite millions of existing variants of $thing", "200.000 barrels of radioactive waste are leaking in the Atlantic", "Tech bro raising millions for a bunch of dotfiles and scripts, aka 'agentic Linux'", and "AI replies to post, trying to empathize, offering attention in form of 'slop reply as a service' for a mere $25 per post read".

Have we fucking lost it, as a species?

Yes, we probably did, despite every half-assed, mostly individual attempt to slow down the decline. As such, our species is probably deserving of every imaginable apocalypse that we'll inevitably get. A shame though, that it won't just be us - we'll destroy everything around us first.

I doubt I'll find better words for it than others already did. Two quotes that cut like a knife:

> Nothing is sacred.<br>
> Every gift we've been given, every resource discovered, each new and shining thing that catches our eye, we pollute.<br>
> Disrespect.<br>
> Violate.<br>
> We tell ourselves this is progress.<br>
> Selling each other the fruits of our destruction.<br>
> Sparing no thought to what we loose or leave behind, churning in our wake.

*Takeshi Kovacs (Anthony Mackie) in Altered Carbon S2E2: "Altered Carbon"*

> I think... humanity is a thin layer of bacteria on a ball of mud hurtling through the void. I think if there was a god, he would've given up on us long ago. He gave us a paradise, and we used everything up. We dug up every ounce of energy and burned it. We consume and excrete, use and destroy. Then we sit here on a neat little pile of ashes, having squeezed anything of value out of this planet, and we ask ourselves, "Why are we here?".<br>
